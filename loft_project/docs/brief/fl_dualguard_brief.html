Federated defense against low-rate flow-table overflow: what the new experiments show

New since 30 Oct: the published papers' own experiments on our setup, namely the comparison against four recent defenses, table-size, timeout and threshold sweeps, benign flash crowds and an adaptive attacker (sections 4–6), plus a flood rule and a switch-eviction fallback. Earlier: local versus federated detectors on Mininet/Ryu, scaling from 3 to 9 controllers and a model-family comparison. Harm throughout means benign flows refused or evicted while active, as a % of benign flows.

Key results

  1. Federation helps on real switches, and only where it should. On Mininet/Ryu, attacks a controller never saw locally harm 13.8 % of benign flows with its local model and 0.36 % with the federated one (D1, attack A2). All 18 testbed runs fall inside or near the simulator's 10-seed range.
  2. Federated detection keeps its lead as controllers are added. With 9 controllers, F1 on attacks seen only by other controllers is 96.1 federated against 52.7 local. The same three controllers gain recall as the federation grows (73 → 89 → 87 at K = 3, 6, 9).
  3. New problem found: plain FedAvg falsely demotes more benign flows at K ≥ 6, mostly on IoT controllers (online harm 8.5–11 % against 2.6–2.9 % for pooled training). Calibrating the threshold on whole flows instead of single polls cuts FedAvg harm to 4.2 % at K = 9 (from 6.9 %), against 2.1 % for pooled. But on the main three-controller setup it leaves harm unchanged and lengthens overflow from 4.7 to 6.6 s (fresh seeds), so it is not the default. IoT controllers remain the open part.
  4. FL-DualGuard beats four recent published defenses on the same simulator. Against rDefender, FloRa, FTOP and FTMaster (and DMS-BTT), v2.1 harms 1.6 % of benign flows against 7.6–23.6 %, and leaves the attacker the smallest share of the table (14.5 % against 22.8–38.0 %), in all 30 controller × seed clusters.
  5. An adaptive attacker breaks v2.1 alone; DualGuard + PortGuard holds. Searching 72 attack settings against each defense, the best response (refresh every 2–3 s with benign-looking packets) harms 54 % of benign flows under v2.1, the worst of seven defenses, against 4.7 % under v2.1 + PortGuard, the best (all 30 clusters). Under a 4× benign flash crowd PortGuard itself harms 13.6 %, so no configuration wins everywhere.
  6. The lead over published defenses holds from 2,000 to 4,000 entries and at idle 20 s. It breaks where the benign traffic alone overflows the table (1,500 entries; OVS eviction is better) and ties at idle 30 s. Letting the switch evict on overflow underneath v2.1 cuts harm from 1.6 to 0.6 % and fixes benign flash crowds (11.3 → 1.7 %).
  7. A per-port flood rule closes the high-rate-flood gap. Under a 2,000 flows/s flood v2.1 harmed 66 % of benign flows; with FloodGuard 2.7 % (rDefender 29 %), and 1.75 % in rDefender's own setting (rDefender 31 %). Caveat: when benign hosts share the attacker's port, A1–A7 harm rises from 1.5 % to 3.0 %.
  8. Offline F1 does not pick the best online detector. In the papers' offline format (8 families, 3 seeds) federated Random Forest and CatBoost lead (F1 98.1 against 97.2 for our MLP). Inside DualGuard on fresh seeds, Random Forest harms 3.6× more benign flows (5.2 % against 1.45 %), though it shortens overflow (1.1 s against 4.3 s). The MLP is also 2.4 KB against 16 MB and 4–8× faster per poll.

Fix tested: calibrate the threshold on whole flows done · 5,544 episodes

Instead of flagging at most 1 % of sampled benign rows, each controller sets its threshold so that at most 1 % of benign flows ever cross it during its validation episodes (each flow's maximum score over all polls). The old row budget turned out to let about 5 % of benign flows cross. Also tested: weighting the federated average by each client's benign rows (favours IoT) or uniformly. Flood rule (FloodGuard) done · preregistered · 2,025 test episodes, unused seeds 14000–14009

Each port's normal rate of new-flow requests is learned in quiet periods. Once the table is 70 % full, a port may install at most max(50, 10 × its normal rate) new entries per second; excess flows are refused at the controller (an OpenFlow meter on the table-miss rule could do this in the switch). Low-rate attacks stay under the cap and are left to v2.1. The 18 candidate settings, the selection rule and four hypotheses were fixed in a hashed file before any run; 10× / 50 / no purge was selected on validation seeds, then tested once.

Threshold sensitivity (E7, validation seeds). Across occupancy triggers 0.5–0.9 and flag budgets 0.5–5 %, attacked harm stays at 1.3–3.4 % and the attacker's share at 12–19 %. PortGuard's caveats from revision 4 stay: it needs attackers on their own access ports, and on the testbed the refused attacker still floods Packet-Ins (24 % harm against fast refresh), so a switch-side meter is needed before claiming it on hardware.
